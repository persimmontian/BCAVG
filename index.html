Dataset BeVo

Project overview

We investigate behavior-conditioned animal vocalization generation: synthesizing animal calls from natural-language descriptions of behaviors, social interactions, and contexts. To address limited behavioral text supervision and substantial variation in vocalization duration, we construct the Behavior-Vocalization Dataset (BeVo), linking 31,191 vocalization events from six species with literature-grounded behavioral conditions. We adapt TangoFlux with Event-Aware variable-length modeling, encoding each vocalization at its actual duration and excluding padded latent positions from attention and the training loss.

We evaluate vocalization discriminability, acoustic distribution fidelity, and language-aligned representation fidelity. Compared with standard fine-tuning, Event-Aware modeling improves vocalization-anchor macro accuracy from 59.86% to 61.99% and reduces Fréchet Audio Distance from 1.40 to 1.22. This demo presents paired real recordings and generated samples, organized by species and conditioning label, with the corresponding prompts and spectrograms.

Event-Aware variable-length modeling

Short animal vocalizations are encoded at their actual durations so that padded positions neither act as valid context nor contribute to the rectified-flow objective.

  1. 01

    Encode actual duration

    Each vocalization is independently encoded with only the padding needed for VAE hop alignment.

  2. 02

    Pad within the minibatch

    Variable-length latent sequences are padded only to the longest event in the current batch.

  3. 03

    Mask attention and loss

    Padding is excluded from transformer context and rectified-flow optimization.

  4. 04

    Generate for the requested duration

    Inference initializes the latent sequence for the target event duration.

AdamW · learning rate 1 × 10−5

50 steps · guidance scale 4.5
Target duration
Corresponding real test recording

The VAE and FLAN-T5 text encoder are frozen; the Flux Transformer, text projection layer, and duration embedder are trained. Both adapted systems share training-sample exposure and condition sampling. The checkpoint with the lowest validation macro loss is selected. Each test instance uses one fixed prompt–duration pair across systems.

Results at a glance

Task-specific adaptation improves all evaluated metrics over zero-shot generation. Event-Aware modeling further improves vocalization discriminability and acoustic fidelity over standard fine-tuning, while language-aligned fidelity metrics remain nearly unchanged.

What this demo shows

Associated conditions

Behavioral conditions describe groups of recordings sharing a vocalization anchor, not verified behavior for each event.

Linguistic generalization

Evaluation uses new formulations of behavioral knowledge represented during training, not entirely unseen behaviors.

Distribution-level fidelity

CLAP-FAD measures species-wise distribution fidelity in language-aligned spaces, not sample-wise prompt correctness.
